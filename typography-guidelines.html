01

Goals for the site

The site supports a Director-level search in Product Marketing and Product Management. It has one job: make a hiring manager believe I turn undervalued features into revenue, retention, and reach, and that I own both the product and the story that sells it.

01

Lead with evidence

The numbers carry the argument. Metrics, outcomes, and sources need to read at a glance and hold up on a second look.

02

Read as editorial, not corporate

The site should feel like a well-set page, with strong type, real hierarchy, and generous rules, rather than a product landing page or a tech deck.

03

Show craft as proof

A career built on typography has to look it. The typesetting itself is evidence of the judgment the work describes.

04

Hold together everywhere

The same system has to work on the site, in a printed résumé, in OG cards, and on a LinkedIn banner viewed on a phone.

02

The typefaces

Three families, three jobs: a serif for voice, a sans for clarity, and a mono for labels and data. Hierarchy comes from family and scale, not from stacking weights.

Do

  • Use Newsreader for anything that carries the argument: headlines, figures, quotes.
  • Keep body copy in Hanken Grotesk at 17px / 1.62.
  • Set mono labels uppercase with .16–.24em tracking.
  • Build hierarchy by changing family or size before changing weight.

Don't

  • Add a fourth family, or a second serif or sans.
  • Set paragraphs or long sentences in mono.
  • Use Newsreader for buttons or navigation.
  • Use faux italics or synthetic bold; load the real styles.
03

Rationale

Each family was chosen for a job the other two could not do. The pairing was tested at the sizes the brand actually ships in.

The briefBefore any family

Three families, three jobs, and nothing decorative.

A career spent selling typography raises the bar for the type it is presented in. The system needed a serif voice for judgment, plain sans prose for long passages, and a monospace channel for labels and data, without the three reading as a costume.

Every candidate also had to be open-licensed and self-hostable. The same set has to render on the site, in a PDF, in a Google Doc, and in a recruiter's email client, without a license question or a fallback that breaks the layout.

NewsreaderDisplay · serif

A serif built for reading on screens.

Newsreader was designed by Production Type for on-screen reading. It has a large x-height, open apertures, and sturdy serifs that hold at small sizes, and it still has editorial character at 138px. That range is why one family carries both the wordmark and the metric figures. Its true italic, drawn rather than slanted, is what lets pull quotes read as testimony.

High-contrast display serifs were the obvious alternative and were set aside. They look good at hero scale and break down everywhere else the mark has to live: at 48px in an avatar, at 11pt on a printed résumé, and in the thin hairlines a laser printer drops.

Hanken GroteskText · sans

Neutral on purpose, so the serif and the numbers stay loud.

The body sans has one job, which is to stay out of the way. Hanken Grotesk is a low-contrast grotesque with an even rhythm and plain letterforms. At 17px a reader gets through a case study without noticing the type, and its 400–700 weight range covers buttons, navigation, and lead-ins without a second family.

The default sans faces used on most product sites were rejected for the reason the Type Default Audit lays out: a typeface everyone uses reads as no decision at all. Hanken is close enough to neutral to disappear in prose and far enough from the defaults to register as a choice.

Spline Sans MonoDetail · mono

The receipts get their own voice.

In this system, monospace marks metadata: a label, a date, an index, a source. Spline Sans Mono is a humanist monospace rather than a terminal face, so it carries that signal without reading as developer tooling. Its fixed-width figures keep numbers aligned, which matters on a site whose argument is made in numbers.

It is used only in uppercase at small sizes with wide tracking. The restriction is deliberate. Once mono shows up in a paragraph, it stops meaning anything.

The pairingHow the three hold

Contrast by family, not by weight.

Hierarchy comes from three clearly different voices at a small number of sizes, rather than many weights of one family. The page stays legible in grayscale and at a glance, survives email clients and document editors that flatten type, and a heading still reads differently from a label when a fallback font is substituted.

All three are open-source variable fonts, which keeps the site light and the print exports faithful.

The governing rule

If a typographic choice can't be defended in a sentence, it doesn't ship. New type enters the system only by replacing something.
